    Don't trust us. Check it.

    You're about to paste client details into a website, so you should be suspicious. Here are four ways to prove nothing leaves your computer. The first one needs no tech skills at all.

    Your text→stays in your browser✕internet, AI companies, us

    1. Turn off your wifi

    Open this page, turn your wifi off, then use it. It works exactly the same, because your text never needs to go anywhere. The line at the top of the page will tell you your wifi is off.

    2. Watch the network

    Press F12 (on a Mac: Cmd + Option + I) and open the Network tab. Paste text and press every button. Nothing new shows up. The page also blocks itself from sending anything, with a security setting (Content Security Policy) set to connect-src 'none'.

    3. Run it from your own computer

    Download Safe Paste as one file and open it from your desktop. No website involved, and it keeps working offline.

    4. Read the code

    The whole tool is one file of plain, readable code. Nothing hidden, nothing scrambled. It's open source, with tests. Hand it to your IT person.

    What we collect: nothing. No accounts, no cookies, no analytics, no ads. Our server keeps no visit logs, and it never receives your text. Your browser only remembers whether you picked light or dark mode, plus your own sign off details if you tick "Remember". Never the text you paste.

    What it can't do

    Safe Paste catches the common patterns: names, phone numbers, emails, SIN, SSN, NHS and National Insurance numbers, health card numbers, dates, addresses and postcodes. It can miss unusual names, nicknames, and details written out in words, like "the tall man who lives above the pharmacy". Always read the safe version before you paste it. Your organization's privacy rules still come first.
